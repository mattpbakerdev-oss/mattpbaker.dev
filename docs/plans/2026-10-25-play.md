# /play: the drivable portfolio

A small low-poly town at `mattpbaker.dev/play`. Visitors drive a chunky car around it, in the
spirit of The Simpsons: Hit & Run, and pull up at buildings that each open one of Matt's
projects. For clients, employers and followers. It should feel crafted, not like a tech demo.

## Constraints

- Static files only, no build step, no backend. Lives in `/play/` in this repo. Nothing else in
  the site changes.
- Three.js 0.170.0 loaded with an import map from
  `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.170.0/build/three.module.js`. No other runtime
  libraries. Physics is a small hand-written arcade model (more controllable than a physics
  engine, and nothing extra to load).
- Every model is built in code from primitives (flat-shaded, low poly). No model files. Sound
  is synthesised with Web Audio. Images and video come from files already on the site.
- Smooth on a mid-range laptop and a modern phone: pixel ratio capped at 1.75, one directional
  light with a 1024 shadow map that follows the car, merged or instanced geometry for repeated
  props, under ~150 draw calls.
- Copy: plain British English, active voice, no em dashes. Never invent facts about the projects;
  all project copy below comes from the existing `/work/*` pages.

## Files

```
play/
  index.html        shell: canvas, loading screen, HUD, panels, touch controls, import map
  play.css          all overlay UI styles
  js/main.js        boot, render loop, wiring
  js/input.js       keyboard + touch → { throttle, steer, brake, interact, horn } state
  js/car.js         car model + arcade physics
  js/physics.js     pure functions: collision (circles vs boxes), ground height, ramps
  js/world.js       terrain, roads, buildings, trees, props, built from data
  js/places.js      DATA: every location, its position, model kind and panel content
  js/extras.js      NPCs, signs, hidden collectibles, knock-over props
  js/ui.js          prompt, panels (dialogs), map/fast travel, HUD, toasts
  js/audio.js       Web Audio synth: engine, horn, bumps, chime, optional music
  test/*.test.mjs   node --test on the pure modules (physics.js, places.js validation, input mapping)
```

Run: `python3 -m http.server` from the repo root, open `http://localhost:8000/play/`.
Test: `node --test play/test/`.

## Driving

Arcade, forgiving, fun. Top speed ~28 units/s, reverse ~10. Acceleration eases in, grip is high
with a little drift at speed, steering tightens as speed rises. The car tilts slightly into
turns and pitches on braking. Collisions with buildings, trees and lamp posts bounce the car
back with a thump sound and a small camera nudge (no nudge with `prefers-reduced-motion`).
Ramps launch the car; gravity brings it back and the suspension squashes on landing. Falling
off the plot is impossible (invisible boundary). If the car flips or gets stuck, R resets it
upright on the nearest road.

Chase camera: behind and above, smoothed position and look-at, pulls back a little with speed.

## Controls

- Desktop: WASD or arrows to drive, Space to brake, E or Enter to interact, H for the horn,
  M for the map, R to reset, Esc to close a panel.
- Phone and tablet (any coarse pointer): a virtual joystick bottom-left (up/down throttle,
  left/right steer) and round buttons bottom-right: interact (only lit near a place), horn.
  Map and mute live in the top bar. Respect safe-area insets.
- First-load "How to play" card showing the right controls for the device, dismissed with any
  key or a button. Stored in localStorage (wrapped in try/catch) so it shows once.

## Places and panels

Driving into a place's amber ring shows a prompt: "Press E to look inside" (or "Tap to look
inside" on touch). Interacting stops the car and opens a panel: an accessible modal dialog
(`role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, labelled, focus moved in and trapped, Esc and a close button
return focus to the game). Game input pauses while a panel is open. On phones the panel is a
bottom sheet; on desktop a right-side card 440px wide, so the town stays visible.

Every place lives in `js/places.js` as data. Adding a project means adding one object:

```js
{
  id: 'up-lock',
  kind: 'project',          // project | about | contact | soon
  model: 'gym',             // which builder in world.js draws it
  position: [x, z], rotation: 0,
  title: 'Up Lock',
  tagline: '...',
  body: ['paragraph', ...],
  stats: [{ value: '~145', label: '...' }],
  media: [{ type: 'image' | 'video' | 'youtube', src, alt, poster }],
  tech: ['Swift', ...],
  links: [{ label, href }],
}
```

A `soon` entry with no content renders the "Coming soon" panel. `test/places.test.mjs` checks
every entry has the required fields for its kind, unique ids, positions on the plot and no two
rings overlapping.

## Extras

- **Signs**: six or so short, funny wooden signs in Matt's voice, readable when the car is near
  (a floating label fades in). Examples: "Speed limit: whatever ships it", "Caution: specs ahead",
  "This road was built by an AI. I reviewed the plan", "Bangkok: 9,500 km that way".
- **Characters**: four blocky townsfolk who walk short loops on the pavements. A speech bubble
  with one line appears when the car is close. If the car hits one, they ragdoll-hop out of the
  way, say "Oi!", and carry on. Never harmed.
- **Knock-over props**: cones, bins, crates and fence panels. Hit them and they tumble with
  simple rigid-body motion (velocity, spin, gravity, ground bounce) then reset to their spot
  out of view after 20 seconds.
- **Breakaway lamp posts and trees** (`breakaway.js` maths, `smash.js` wiring, `spots.js`
  placement): every lamp post and every tree inside the plot breaks away when the car hits it
  (the rim trees, hills and buildings stay solid). They live in `world.breakables`, not
  `world.colliders`; placement, R-reset and fast travel use `world.clearance()` (solid plus
  whatever is still standing). Their seeded placement (with the grass patches) is pure, in
  `spots.js`, which `world.js` draws and the tests hit, so the tests can't go stale. The car
  hits with its body (a box 2.2 by 4.06, bumpers included), swept across the frame; a hit
  counts when the car is heading into it, judged at the point of the sweep nearest the object,
  so a fast glancing hit at a low frame rate still lands.
  Hit & Run style: struck objects pop forward, up and off to one side, never visibly inside the
  car, and land looking solid.
  - **Topple** (below a launch speed of about 8 for a lamp, 13 to 15 for a tree, in units per
    second): a hinge fall over its base that speeds up, with a small bounce where it lands. It
    falls the car's way, turned 35% towards the side of the car's centreline it was on (a coin
    toss for a dead-centre hit); the base is shoved at 0.6 of the car's velocity. The car loses
    35% of its speed to a lamp post and 60% to a tree, so it doesn't roll on into what it just
    pushed over. A lamp turns its arm side-on as it falls; a tree rests on its crown. A car
    slower than 4 that catches one still falling pushes it along with its bumper (no faster
    than the car's own step plus 0.05 a frame) rather than kicking it.
  - **Launch** (above it): forward speed `max(fling + swing, speed * (1 - scrub) + 9 (lamp) or 7
    (tree))`, capped at 38, so it always beats the car's speed after the hit; sideways 0.25 of
    that for a dead-centre hit rising to 0.5 off the very edge of the car, towards the side of
    the car it was on (coin toss if dead centre), with a yaw spin of up to 1.5 rad/s on glancing
    hits; lift `(5.5 + 0.3 * (speed - launch speed)) * sqrt(k)`; tumbling end over end about the
    axis square to its flight line (a lamp makes about 1.3 turns, give or take 15%, by the time
    it drops back to launch height, so it never lands on its foot; a tree spins
    `(2.5 + 0.28 * speed) * k`, k = sqrt(2 / mass)). The car loses 10% to a lamp post and 30% to
    a tree. Each hard landing scrubs speed; a lamp post's landings scrub harder and it drags
    along the ground, so it slides about half as far as a box would; one that lands near upright
    (or on its head) is tipped straight over and never skates on an end. It settles lying on its
    side, never on an end. A launched tree rolls (0.35 s, ease in-out, still sliding) into the
    toppled resting pose on its first landing slower than 4 along the ground: trunk base on the
    ground where it lies, crown on the ground beyond (unless it lies across a ramp's edge).
  - **Kick again**: if the car drives into anything knocked (fallen, still falling, or flying
    slower than the car), it is sent on again. One lying down (or settling, or falling) is
    shoved along the ground on its hinge, base and crown staying down: at least 4 faster than
    the car along its line and off to the side its middle is on (8 for a tree, 4 for a lamp),
    swinging round at 3.5 rad/s towards the car's line on its trailing end (the one already
    off to the side), the end across the car's path going first, so a kicked tree clears the car's line within about half a second.
    All of that scales down below a car speed of 4, so a crawling car nudges rather than flings.
    One still flying is sent on, with no lift. The car loses 5%; no kick sound within 0.3 s of
    the strike or the last kick. And a last line of defence: any visible part of a knocked
    object found inside the car's body (a canopy above the bonnet, which is 1.4 high over the
    front 1.3, doesn't count) is slid on ahead of the bumper, at most 0.45 a frame.
  - **Solid to the car**: debris that can't be shoved clear because a wall is in the way is slid
    sideways along the wall towards the side with more room; whatever still overlaps stops the
    car short of it. Below the kick speed (1) knocked ones are solid to the car, and so is a
    standing one while the car creeps into it below 0.2; in both cases speed still builds, so
    pressing on kicks or knocks it.
  - **Buildings and the plot**: each body carries sample points (both ends and the middle of
    its length, the pole and head, or the trunk and canopy). In the air only a real building's
    walls (`WALLS` in `places.js`, plus 0.6) push them, so nothing hangs against a garden's
    invisible edge, and spin and gravity carry on through a push; a wall's bounce never throws a
    body back at the car close behind it. On the ground the whole building plot (not building
    sites) pushes, easing a body off at up to 9 units a second, and the town's edge holds hard;
    at rest no sample is in either. A falling top catches and leans only on a real building's
    walls, never on an open forecourt or garden; low solids (benches, fountain, signs) and the
    plot edge never catch it, so it falls past or over them and lies flat.
  - Feedback: a camera nudge that lurches towards the car's travel, a synthesised clang (lamp)
    or woody crack (tree). On a phone (coarse pointer, narrow screen) a launch at 14 or more
    holds for about three frames (50 ms at a fifth speed) so the impact reads; never on desktop
    or under reduced motion. A knocked lamp flickers and goes out; the dead head is cool grey
    (`#77808a`), unlit glass. A knocked tree stops counting as a camera occluder. Flying pieces
    pass through townsfolk and props. Each one goes home 20 seconds later, once neither its spot
    nor where it lies is on screen and the car is clear of the spot.
  - Reduced motion: nothing launches. Every hit topples, the fall turned 55 degrees towards
    the glancing side, the base shoved at `min(0.6 * speed, 5)` along the car's travel plus 4
    sideways (a slide under about 2 units), no spin, no bounce, no air time; a kick-again is a
    shove along the ground with no swing. The car loses the same speed as the full-motion hit
    would cost (a launch's loss for what would have launched). The lamp goes out at once, the
    camera doesn't nudge and there is no hit-stop.
  - Drawn by moving the existing instances, so no extra draw calls.
- **Hidden extras**: four collectibles tucked away (behind the gym, on top of a ramp jump, in
  the park, behind the building sites): a golden dumbbell, a film reel, a graduation cap, a
  mini tuk-tuk. Each spins and glows faintly. Driving through one plays a chime and a toast
  with a one-line note (e.g. tuk-tuk: "Bangkok, next. Two weeks' notice and I'm on the plane").
  HUD counter "Found 1 of 4", saved in localStorage. Finding all four shows a small celebration
  and a line from Matt.
- **Horn**: a cartoon double honk. Townsfolk turn to look.

## Map and fast travel

M (or the Map button) opens a dialog: a top-down drawing of the town with each place labelled,
and a list of buttons beneath. Choosing one drops the car outside it facing the door. This is
also the keyboard and screen-reader route to every panel.

## Loading screen

Warm dark background, "Matt Baker" wordmark, "Loading Mattsville" and a thin amber progress
bar. Then a "Start driving" button (needed anyway to unlock audio). A small "Prefer the normal
site? mattpbaker.dev" link.

## Accessibility and motion

- All overlay UI keyboard reachable, visible amber focus ring, labelled buttons, contrast AA.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no camera shake, no bobbing markers (static), no celebration
  particles; driving itself stays.
- Canvas has `aria-label` describing the game and pointing at the Map for a list of places.
- If WebGL is unavailable, show a friendly card with a link to the normal site.
