Listing Check

A pre-publish checker for Bangkok rental agents. Paste a draft listing and it scores completeness out of 100 with per-field fixes, benchmarks the rent per square metre against comparable listings, and offers an AI rewrite in English and Thai that may only use facts from the draft. Built in about 12 to 16 hours from a one-page spec, with the build log kept from the first commit.

Sonnet was as clean, a third faster and a third of the price, so it is the default. The one Opus addition was genuine: a draft that said "furnished" came back "fully furnished", which is exactly the failure the guardrail exists to catch.

What the AI got wrong, and how it was fixed

The fact checker was wrong before the model was. At run time it flagged five of the first twenty Opus rewrites. Four of those were its own fault: the drafts said "pool view" and the model wrote "looks out over the pool", which the concept matcher treated as a new pool claim. The matcher now treats a view of something as evidence the thing exists, the logged outputs were re-scored without calling the model again, and a planted test keeps the fix fixed: "pool view" in, "looks out over the pool" out, nothing flagged.

My benchmark rule lost to the measured one. I specified a price benchmark that matched on bedrooms. Measured on the sample, it was worse than the original: clean listings wrongly flagged rose from 16% to 28%, and mispriced listings caught fell from 50 to 46 of 51. The chain that shipped compares the same building and bedrooms, then area, tier and bedrooms, then area and tier, then the whole area, because building tier was what actually separated a listing from its comparables. The spec was updated to say so.

The agent reported the site done while the live URL showed the old build. I opened the production site and saw a white page with none of the new work. Local main was two commits ahead of origin and neither had been pushed; the report had said "done" and nothing in it said undeployed. Fixed by pushing and redeploying, then a rule went into the log: done means committed, pushed and, for anything visible, verified on the live URL. A report that stops at the commit has to say so in its first line.
