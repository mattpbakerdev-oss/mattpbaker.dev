Up Lock

An iPhone app that blocks distracting apps until you do a set of push-ups, squats or jumping jacks. The camera counts reps on the phone itself and a finished set unlocks the app for a timed window, with daily limits, streaks, stats and a subscription. For anyone who wants their phone to cost them something.

What the AI got wrong, and how it was fixed

The pose model does not know what a push-up is. Apple's Vision model gives you joints, not exercises, so the first version counted anything that bent an elbow, including standing in front of the phone and waving. A rep is now defined in terms the model understands: elbow angle mapped to a 0 to 1 signal, which has to drop below 0.35 and rise above 0.70, with a dead band in between so jitter cannot flip it and a half-second floor so a twitch cannot double count.

The first anti-cheat rule overcorrected. To prove you were horizontal it required the hips to be visible. Real push-ups then stopped counting, because when the phone is on the floor your legs are out of frame. Fixed on 25 June: standing is rejected only when a visible cue positively shows it, a torso near vertical or a nose well above the shoulders, and never because a joint is missing. The rule that broke real reps became the test pushupArmsOnlyNoHipsCountsTheRep.

Each rule has a test, named for the thing it stops. pushupStandingFakeIsRejected, pushupArmsOnlyNoHipsCountsTheRep and deadBandJitterDoesNotCount pin the three rules above, so a change to the counter that re-breaks one of them fails before it ships. Rep counting runs on the phone and nothing is uploaded.
