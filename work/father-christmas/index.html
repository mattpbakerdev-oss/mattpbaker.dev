Father Christmas Video

A personalised video from a British Father Christmas, written for one child by name. A parent writes a short letter: first name, friends, three good things from the year and one wish. Father Christmas reads back a script made from it, and never promises a present. Two styles, Animated at £4.99 and Painted at £7.99, paid once, with a printable Nice List certificate. No accounts, no photos, first names only, and everything is deleted after Christmas.

Live in test mode; launching for Christmas 2026.

See it

52 seconds each, with sound and captions. Both are real renders from the production pipeline, made for Isla, a made-up child.

What the AI got wrong, and how it was fixed

Pattern rules could not guard the script. The script must never invent a person or promise a present. Two rounds of regex rules failed both ways: they flagged ordinary praise like "Brave" and missed invented names and gift promises worded a new way. A deterministic checker now stays as a backstop, and Claude Haiku reads every script as a judge for invented people and promised presents, at about £0.0002 a script.

The first live order rendered but never arrived. fal lip-synced all seven parts, then the ffmpeg step that joins them hit Vercel's 300-second limit, and on retry it ran out of memory. The parts are now joined one at a time with ffmpeg held to two threads. The next live order was delivered end to end.

The cheap lip-sync model could not find a cartoon face. LatentSync could not reliably detect the painted Father Christmas. A semi-realistic 3D version it could detect had lip-sync too poor to sell. A higher-end model, Kling Avatar, cost about $3.37 a video, too much at these prices. Both styles moved to fal Boreal at about $0.60 a video.
